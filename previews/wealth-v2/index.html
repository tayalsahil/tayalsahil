01 / The premise

How rich
does a life
feel?

One ledger is never enough.A working proposition, not an accounting rule

A balance sheet can tell us what we own and owe. It cannot tell us whether Tuesday belongs to us, whether our relationships are reciprocal, or whether curiosity still has room to breathe.

Money matters. It buys shelter, treatment, opportunity and freedom from certain constraints. Pretending otherwise would romanticise hardship. But money is one form of capital among several that shape what a life can become.

Here, wealth means resources that sustain choice, care, learning and meaningful experience. Some are material. Some are relational. Some fluctuate with health and circumstance. None should be converted into a made-up numerical score.

A body of work is evidence of effort, not a guaranteed return.

Agency

Options matter more than the appearance of independence.

OPEN THE FOLIO +

Intermission / Reality check

No amount of metaphor
pays the electricity bill.

This is not an argument that financial resources are optional. Income, savings and financial stability remain vital. Hold two truths at once: material security deserves serious attention, and a human life cannot be reduced to its market valuation.

THE RISK OF ONLY COUNTING MONEY

We overlook care, capability, liberty and ordinary contentment.

THE RISK OF IGNORING MONEY

We mistake precariousness for freedom—and potential for a livelihood.
